<Traffic Interaction Types (Reference)>
When describing interactions, you may reference these standard types:

- **Distance keeping (DK):** Ego adjusts speed to maintain safe following distance behind lead vehicle
- **Yielding at intersection for crossing path conflict (YCP):** Ego slows/stops to let conflicting agent pass whose path crosses ego's intended path
- **Yielding at intersection for merging path conflict (YMP):** Ego yields to agent merging into same target lane from different entry
- **Proceeding after conflict resolved (P):** Ego accelerates through intersection after conflicting agent clears or gap opens
- **Proactive deceleration (PD):** Ego slows down preventatively before conflict materializes
- **Reactive braking (RB):** Ego brakes hard in response to imminent collision threat
- **Hesitation (H):** Ego decelerates and re-accelerates, showing uncertainty about right-of-way

You may also define new interaction types if the behavior doesn't fit these categories.
</Traffic Interaction Types (Reference)>

### Step 2: Analyze Representative Behavior (Medoid Trial)
Examine the BEV snapshots and action log:
- What does the ego vehicle do? (Key maneuvers and timing)
- How do other agents behave?
- Where is the conflict point? (Which intersection region, which lanes?)
- What is the critical decision moment? (Use MFPCA heatmap for guidance)

Conflict point: center of intersection where left-turn path crosses oncoming straight path.
MFPCA heatmap shows high variation at t=2-3s (decision to yield or proceed)."

### Step 3: Identify Behavioral Pattern
Synthesize Steps 1-2 to name the cluster's defining behavior:
- What makes this cluster distinct from others?
- Is this a cautious strategy? Aggressive? Hesitant?
- Does the ego yield early, late, or not at all?

Example:
"This cluster represents a 'Proactive Yield' strategy.
Ego detects conflict early and decelerates well before reaching conflict point.
Distinct from aggressive clusters (ego proceeds without slowing) and from
reactive clusters (ego brakes hard at last moment).
Ego's deceleration is gradual and starts 2-3 seconds before predicted conflict."

### Step 4: Safety Assessment
Evaluate risks and failure modes:
- Why do collisions occur in this cluster (if collision rate > 0%)?
- What conditions lead to near-misses (low TTC)?
- Is this a fundamentally safe or unsafe strategy?

Example:
"Failure mode: Collisions occur when oncoming speed > 65 kph AND start delay < 1.0s.
In these cases, even early yielding is insufficient because oncoming vehicle arrives
before ego can fully stop. TTC drops below 1.0s.
Safe range: Start delay > 1.5s OR oncoming speed < 55 kph ensures TTC > 2.0s."

### Step 5: Parameter Conditions
Identify when this behavior is triggered:
- What parameter combinations lead to this cluster?
- What is the "safe operating range" vs "failure boundary"?

Example:
"Trigger condition: Oncoming speed 50–70 kph AND start delay 0.5–2.0s
Safe range: Start delay > 1.5s OR oncoming speed < 55 kph
Risky boundary: Start delay < 1.0s AND oncoming speed > 65 kph (collision risk 15%)"
