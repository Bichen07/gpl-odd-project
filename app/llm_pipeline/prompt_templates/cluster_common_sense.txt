<Traffic Safety Principles>
When analyzing driving behavior clusters, apply these principles:

1. **Right-of-way at intersections:**
   - Vehicles on the priority road (main road) have right-of-way
   - Left-turning vehicles must yield to oncoming straight/right-turning traffic
   - When paths conflict, the vehicle that arrives first typically proceeds first
   - However, defensive driving may prioritize safety over strict right-of-way

2. **Safe following distance:**
   - Maintain 2-second time headway as a minimum safe following distance
   - Greater distances required at higher speeds or in adverse conditions
   - TTC (Time-To-Collision) < 1.5 seconds indicates critical near-miss
   - TTC < 1.0 seconds indicates imminent collision risk

3. **Anticipatory driving:**
   - Experienced drivers anticipate conflicts early and adjust proactively
   - Reactive strategies (late braking) increase collision risk
   - Hesitation and uncertainty can create dangerous situations

4. **Speed management:**
   - Ego should decelerate when approaching potential conflict zones
   - Rapid deceleration (harsh braking) indicates inadequate anticipation
   - Smooth deceleration indicates well-calibrated threat assessment

5. **Intersection navigation:**
   - Ego should scan for conflicting agents before entering intersection
   - Commitment point: once entering, ego should proceed decisively
   - Stopping mid-intersection is dangerous (creates new conflicts)
</Traffic Safety Principles>

<Cluster Analysis Mindset>
Unlike single-trial interaction detection, cluster analysis focuses on:

- **Pattern recognition:** What behavioral strategy unites these trials?
- **Variability:** Where do trials within the cluster diverge? (use MFPCA heatmap)
- **Failure mode identification:** Why do some trials in cluster fail while others succeed?
- **Parameter sensitivity:** Which scenario parameters most influence safety outcomes?

Key questions to ask:
- "Is this cluster defined by ego's strategy or by environmental conditions?"
- "Do failures occur due to poor decision-making or impossible situations?"
- "What distinguishes this cluster from adjacent clusters in the embedding space?"
</Cluster Analysis Mindset>

<Behavioral Pattern Taxonomy>
Common cluster archetypes in intersection scenarios:

**Safe/Defensive Patterns:**
- **Proactive Yielding:** Early detection → gradual deceleration → long wait
- **Conservative Gaps:** Only proceed when large time gaps (> 3s) available
- **Defensive Stop:** Full stop even when legal right-of-way, to ensure safety

**Moderate-Risk Patterns:**
- **Reactive Yielding:** Late detection → rapid braking → short wait
- **Threshold-Based:** Proceed if gap exceeds minimum threshold, yield otherwise
- **Speed-Dependent:** Strategy varies based on oncoming vehicle speed

**High-Risk Patterns:**
- **Aggressive Proceed:** Minimal or no yielding despite conflict
- **Hesitation:** Decelerate then accelerate before committing (indecisive)
- **Misjudged Gap:** Attempt to cross/merge but timing is insufficient
- **Forced Yield:** Right-of-way violated by oncoming agent, ego forced to brake hard

Use these as inspiration, but create cluster-specific labels based on actual data.
</Behavioral Pattern Taxonomy>
