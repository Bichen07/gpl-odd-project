<Your Task>
INITIAL-CONDITION matched pair: two sides with nearly identical scenario inputs
(OncomingSpeed, OncomingStartDelay; param_dist ≤ caliper) from different embedding
clusters. Explain whether the cluster split is justified or over-fine.

**Cluster provenance matters:** the embedding clusters were produced by applying
MFPCA followed by HDBSCAN to the **whole trajectory**. Therefore, membership
primarily reflects whole-trajectory shape, duration, and collision termination;
it does not guarantee one clean interaction intent. Two matched boundary trials
can have very similar trajectories while differing at the conflict decision:
one may attempt to go through and collide, while the other may brake to yield
but still collide. Treat that as a possible collision-boundary mixture to test,
not as an annotation error and not as evidence that both sides share one motive.

PARAMETER-SPACE params are sampled OpenSCENARIO inputs (Oncoming start/target speed and start
delay) — NOT measured speed at peak conflict.

Matched parameter-space sides often MOVE VERY SIMILARLY. Your job is to find what still differs —
**persistent geometry, longitudinal relationship, minimum distance between vehicle
boundaries, and named vehicle position in Ego coordinates** —
across shared-clock phases, using the pair context + synced BEV frames. Cluster
medoid `output/medoid_trial.yaml` extracts are a **final consistency check only**, never evidence for these
boundary sides.
Do not stop at "both collide" or "both decelerate." Do not treat a 0.3 s brake-onset
gap or an oscillatory speed-difference sample as the critical divergence unless
minimum distance between vehicle boundaries or longitudinal relationship also
splits and stays split.

Metric nouns in context are plain and precise: Ego speed, named vehicle speed,
center-to-center distance, named vehicle azimuth relative to Ego heading,
center-distance closing/opening rate, estimated time to collision, named vehicle position

<Separation decision rubric (use exactly one label)>
Judge the TWO BOUNDARY SIDES against each other. Cluster medoids are a later
comment, not a veto. Family = **yield-family** (decelerate/stop to give way;
named vehicle not behind, including failed overlap while still braking) vs
**pass-family** (go-through: named vehicle behind, hold/accel into the gap, or
brake_release then continue). Do not put overlap-while-braking in the pass
family. Do not call `inconclusive` because a boundary YAML string differs from
its cluster medoid.

1) `over_fine` (merge signal):
- both boundary sides are the same family, AND
- outcomes match (both collision or both safe/near-miss), AND
- no persistent geometry split beyond tiny late clearance.

2) `justified` (keep split) — at least one is true:
  a) different family across sides (yield-family vs pass-family) or different
     motive family, regardless of whether outcomes match, OR
  b) same family, outcomes differ, and a real persistent clearance / geometry
     delta is the contact vs near-miss difference (even if late / small), OR
  c) same family, same outcome, but a persistent stay-ahead vs overlap split
     (not just tiny late clearance).

3) `inconclusive` (cannot call justified or over_fine):
- evidence is contradictory or missing.
Do NOT use `inconclusive` for medoid mismatch. Do NOT use it for a tiny late
clearance split when both sides share geometry family — that is `over_fine`
(same outcome) or `justified` 2b (outcome differs). `inconclusive` here is the
pair card's call; cluster_summary maps it to neighbor `verdict: inconclusive`
(different field, same word on purpose).

Do not invent new labels. If you suspect finer sub-structure, keep
`separation_call` within the 3 labels and state the split/subdivide concern in
`separation_reason` and `hypothesis`.
</Separation decision rubric>
