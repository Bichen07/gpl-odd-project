<Your Task>
Two trials with similar scenario parameter inputs (OncomingSpeed, OncomingStartDelay; param_dist ≤ caliper). They sit in different clusters because MFPCA + HDBSCAN grouped the whole trajectory. Similar input, different trajectory over time. Find that difference. 

`behavior_similarity` is the behavior of these two boundary trials, not of the whole clusters. The call and its one-line reason are defined in <Behavior call>. Write them last.

Whole-trajectory membership does not guarantee one interaction. Two matched trials can look alike and still differ at the conflict: one goes through and collides, the other brakes to yield and also collides. That mix is a boundary to describe, not an annotation error, and not evidence that both sides share one resolution.

The scenario parameters are sampled inputs (oncoming speed and start delay), not the measured speed at the conflict.

Find the earliest time the two trajectories start to differ, and what differs then. It can be a behavior (for example one brakes 0.3 s earlier), the body gap, who is ahead, the position in Ego coordinates, or the speed. Keep it only if the two trajectories stay apart after that time. A single sample that returns to the same path is noise. Each side's medoid card is a last comment, never the evidence for that side. Do not stop at "both collide" or "both decelerate."

The pair file also prints Ego's average speed during the previous 1 second. Use that phrase as printed. Nouns are defined in the glossary.

Name sides `[cA]` / `[cB]` (for example `[c0]`, `[c4]`).
</Your Task>

<Behavior call (use exactly one label)>
Judge the TWO BOUNDARY SIDES against each other. Cluster medoids are a later
comment, not a veto. Family = **yield-family** (decelerate/stop to give way;
named vehicle not behind, including failed overlap while still braking) vs
**pass-family** (go-through: named vehicle behind, hold/accel into the gap, or
brake_release then continue). Do not put overlap-while-braking in the pass
family. Do not call `inconclusive` because a boundary YAML string differs from
its cluster medoid.

1) `similar` (same behavior family and same outcome; the trajectory split remains):
- both boundary sides are the same family, AND
- outcomes match (both collision or both safe/near-miss), AND
- the remaining difference is a small late clearance or a whole-trajectory
  detail rather than a different resolution.
  Say what that remaining difference is. This is not a request to merge.

2) `distinct` (the boundary sides differ in behavior or outcome) — at least one is true:
  a) different family across sides (yield-family vs pass-family), regardless of
     whether outcomes match, OR
  b) same family and outcomes differ (safe versus collision, or contact versus
     a near-miss). Cite the lasting clearance when the context has one. When
     the context names no lasting split, the reason says so and the call stays
     `distinct`, OR
  c) same family, same outcome, but a persistent stay-ahead vs overlap split
     (not just tiny late clearance).

3) `inconclusive` (cannot call distinct or similar):
- evidence is contradictory or missing.
Do NOT use `inconclusive` for medoid mismatch. Do NOT use it for a tiny late
clearance split when both sides share geometry family — that is `similar`
(same outcome) or `distinct` 2b (outcome differs). `inconclusive` here is the
pair card's call; the cluster summary copies it onto that neighbor's
`behavior_similarity`.

Do not invent a fourth label. If the two sides also differ inside one cluster, keep the three labels and describe that mix in `behavior_similarity_reason` and `hypothesis`.
</Behavior call>
