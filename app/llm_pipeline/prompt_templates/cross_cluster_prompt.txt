<Your Task>
You are a traffic safety expert evaluating whether a set of driving-scenario clusters forms a *good* behavioral partition.

You will receive:
1. A **narrative** summary of each cluster (archetype label + behavior description from
   `cluster_summary.yaml` / medoid fallback). No raw `cluster.json` stats.
2. Medoid cards — the per-cluster medoid analysis, including its `primary_motive` from the closed motive set and the timestamped evidence for it.
3. Boundary trial descriptions — pairs of trials that sit right at the border between two adjacent clusters, showing the most similar-yet-different behaviors found.
4. parameter-space-matched pair cards — pairs of trials from *different* clusters whose scenario inputs are nearly identical (param_dist ≤ tau). These are the strongest available test of a boundary: if two trials start almost the same and end in different clusters with different outcomes, the boundary is behavioral rather than an artifact.
5. A neighbor rollup — each cluster's OWN localized distinct/similar/ambiguous verdict against its actual parameter-space-matched neighbors, already produced per-cluster (Stage B's cluster-summary pass). Treat this as prior work already done at the local level: cite it and reconcile it with the global view rather than re-deriving each pairwise judgment from scratch.
6. Deterministic **selection-eval findings** already computed offline from artifacts (purity, motive distinctness, pair flips, merge candidates, selection_score). Treat these as measurements, not opinions: do not contradict a number, explain what it implies. Do not recompute rates from raw tables — you are not given `cluster.json`.
7. A question: **Are the clusters behaviorally distinct and well-separated?**

Your task is to:
1. Rate the **overall behavioral separation** between clusters (1–10).
2. Rate the **boundary clarity** — how easy it is for a domain expert to tell the clusters apart (1–10).
3. For every cluster, state its **behavioral archetype** in ≤5 words.
4. Identify any clusters that are **too similar** and might be candidates for merging.
5. Identify any cluster whose **intra-cluster consistency is too low** (inconsistent members).
6. Recommend the single most useful change to the partition, or confirm it should be kept.
7. Provide a short overall **inter_notes** paragraph summarizing the findings.

Two failure modes matter. Over-splitting: two clusters tell the same behavioral story, so the extra boundary buys nothing an analyst can act on. Under-splitting: one cluster mixes behaviors a reviewer would want named apart (e.g. passes-then-brakes lumped with passes-and-keeps-speed).

A high silhouette does NOT settle this. Silhouette measures geometry in FPC space; you are judging whether the clusters are behaviorally distinct and whether their boundaries hold under matched initial conditions.
</Your Task>

<Scoring Guide>
behavioral_separation_score (1–10):
  10 = every cluster has a completely distinct driving strategy (e.g., C0=pass-first, C1=proactive-yield, C2=collision, C3=emergency-brake)
   7 = mostly distinct, one pair shows some overlap in behavior
   5 = two or more clusters are very similar in strategy, only differing in timing
   1 = all clusters look like the same behavior with minor variation

boundary_clarity_score (1–10):
  10 = given only the descriptions, a domain expert could immediately separate all clusters with zero ambiguity
   7 = mostly clear; one boundary is fuzzy
   5 = at least two clusters require careful analysis to distinguish
   1 = descriptions are so similar that assignment seems arbitrary
</Scoring Guide>

<Mandatory Chain Of Thought>
### Step 1: Read each cluster archetype
List each cluster ID, its narrative archetype / caption, and medoid primary_motive.
Note any that appear similar. Do not invent collision rates — use Deterministic Checks
and medoid/summary outcomes when needed.

### Step 2: Group clusters by primary_motive
Two clusters sharing a motive is a *signal* of over-splitting, not proof — they may differ in timing or magnitude, which is a legitimate reason to keep them apart. For every repeated motive, name the dimension that still separates the clusters (timing, magnitude, outcome, parameter region), or say they are redundant.

### Step 3: Compare adjacent boundary pairs
For each boundary trial pair provided:
- Do the two trials represent qualitatively different strategies, or are they near-identical?
- What is the smallest parameter difference that produced the behavioral divergence?
- Does this boundary seem natural and meaningful, or arbitrary?

### Step 4: Weigh the parameter-space-matched pairs
For each Parameter-space pair, state whether it supports the boundary (outcome flip, or clearly different behavior under near-identical inputs) or undermines it (same outcome AND same story). Cite the pair folder. Parameter-range overlap near 1.0 means clusters occupy the same initial conditions, so behavior rather than scenario setup is what separates them — that is expected, not a defect.

### Step 5: Reconcile the neighbor rollup
Read the <Neighbor Rollup> block. For each cluster's local verdicts, check whether your own Step 2-4 reading agrees. Where they agree, cite the rollup instead of re-arguing it. Where they disagree, say so explicitly and state which reading you trust more and why (e.g. the local pass only saw its own Parameter-space pairs; the global pass can see the whole partition).

### Step 6: Rate separation and clarity
Using the scoring guide, assign scores with justification. Boundary clarity should track what the parameter-space-matched pairs and the neighbor rollup showed.

### Step 7: Identify merge candidates
If any two clusters have overlapping behavior descriptions AND their boundary or Parameter-space pairs look nearly identical, flag them as merge candidates. A "similar" neighbor-rollup verdict for a pair is supporting evidence, not proof by itself.

### Step 8: Identify low-consistency / mixed clusters
Use Deterministic Checks (outcome purity / mixed_outcome findings) and narrative
captions that admit mixed behavior. Flag candidates for split when the story is
not one behavior. Do not invent intra_consistency or collision_rate numbers.
</Mandatory Chain Of Thought>
